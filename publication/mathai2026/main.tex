\begin{abstract}
Can a language model learn to use exact machinery as neuron-shaped internal
computation rather than as an external tool? We replace 28 existing
intermediate coordinates in one frozen Qwen2.5-0.5B MLP with a typed interface
to a zero-parameter decimal adder. A 25,088-weight interface learns route,
operand, digit, and result representations; the model width and all pretrained
weights remain unchanged. Across three independently trained interfaces, a
frozen final audit yields 173/180 exact natural-language additions, 174/180
exact calculator trajectories, and 180/180 token-exact preserved adversarial
negatives. Ablating calculator-result coordinates leaves 9/180 correct.
Untouched Qwen returns 1/60 exact requested numerals on the same prompts and
token budget. The compound protocol still fails two prespecified gates due to
six operand-framing errors and one downstream decoding error. These results
provide causal evidence for a narrow deterministic-neuron mechanism while
separating exact execution from fallible learned routing and decoding.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Language models are flexible probabilistic predictors; arithmetic is a rigid
state-transition process. Neural arithmetic units and structured training can
improve extrapolation \citep{trask2018,jelassi2023,cho2024}, while compiled
transformers and neural algorithmic reasoners study how known algorithms can
coexist with learned representations \citep{lindner2023,bounsi2024}. The
Integrated Gated Calculator (IGC) is the closest direct precedent: it learns
input, routing, and output maps around a non-differentiable four-operation
calculator in a frozen Llama 3.1 8B model \citep{dietz2025}. We therefore do
\emph{not} claim the broad integrated-calculator idea as novel.

We test a narrower architectural question: can deterministic semantics occupy
selected coordinates of an existing transformer MLP, with surrounding learned
computation treating them like an internal neural interface? Unlike an
output-side correction, the result must pass through the model's ordinary MLP
down projection and seven downstream decoder blocks. Our contributions are:
(i) an in-place 28-coordinate calculator interface in frozen
Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct \citep{qwen2024}; (ii) three-seed, same-prompt evaluation
of arithmetic, routing, preservation, and causality; and (iii) explicit
retention of failed audits and a negative compound verdict.

\section{In-place calculator coordinates}

Qwen's gated MLP at decoder layer 16 produces
\[
y_{\mathrm{MLP}}=W_{\mathrm{down}}
\bigl(\operatorname{SiLU}(W_{\mathrm{gate}}h)\odot W_{\mathrm{up}}h\bigr).
\]
We reserve 28 of its 4,864 intermediate coordinates after a non-arithmetic
impact census. Sixteen learned rows classify a hidden vector into route
(2 coordinates), token role (3), and digit/non-digit type (11). Consistent
high-confidence role and digit symbols populate two operand registers. A
frozen decimal ripple-carry cell returns one of 12 result symbols
(digits 0--9, end, pad), represented by 12 learned columns of
$W_{\mathrm{down}}$. The remaining 4,836 channels and every pretrained weight
are frozen. Table~\ref{tab:params} gives the exact learned budget.

Generation-time microcircuit state latches the first route, captures the first
valid operands, and advances a result-position counter. This makes registered
state exact after capture, but it is \emph{not} residual-native recurrent
memory. Each response supports one addition call.

\section{Controlled evaluation}

\paragraph{Model and training.}
We pin Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct to a recorded immutable revision, freeze all
494,032,768 pretrained parameters, and greedily decode. Three interface
initializations use seeds 13,201--13,203 and identical data. Supervised labels
teach route, role, digit, and result columns; the system does not discover the
ABI from answer reward alone.

\paragraph{Adaptive development and frozen audit.}
An initial frozen audit obtained 58/60 correct additions per seed but falsely
routed 17/180 adversarial negatives. Retraining only the 1,792 route-row
weights eliminated false routes on two new exact-string-disjoint audits. A
later audit exposed sequence-time operand drift; adding the deterministic
response-local register fixed that failure. All unsuccessful runs remain in
the record. The final register audit was frozen before generation and uses, per
seed, 30 direct additions, 30 word problems, and 60 adversarial negatives from
new families. This is a post-audit confirmation of a revised architecture,
not an independent external replication.

\paragraph{Measures.}
We score exact numeral-only response, first-step operand register, exact
digit-plus-end calculator trajectory, paired result ablation, false routing,
and token-exact preservation relative to untouched Qwen. A retrospective base
comparison uses the identical 60 prompts and eight-token greedy budget; a
base-only 64-token sensitivity also records the final integer.

Table~\ref{tab:results} shows replicated high accuracy and strong causal
dependence. All 174 exact first-step registers remained stable and generated
the exact deterministic calculator trajectory. The six input failures came
from two direct prompt instances shared across all seeds, indicating a prompt
representation weakness rather than arithmetic error. Seed 1 had one further
output error: exact internal symbols for $0+0$ were decoded downstream as
``I.'' Thus no observed calculator transition was wrong given exact typed
state, but the learned interface was not infallible.

Untouched Qwen returned the requested numeral on 1/60 prompts under the same
eight-token budget. With 64 tokens and the looser last-integer measure, it
recovered 27/60. The implant seeds solved 57/60, 58/60, and 58/60; the two best
seeds solved every prompt recovered by the loose base measure plus 31 more.
This comparison is retrospective and is not a frozen protocol gate.

The compound verdict is negative. Three of five final gates pass. Mean exact
accuracy is 57.67/60 against a frozen 58/60 threshold, and the single
downstream decoding error violates the all-exact conditional-output gate.

\section{Discussion}

The narrow mechanism works: learned language-model activations can supply
typed inputs to deterministic coordinates inside an existing MLP; exact result
symbols can survive ordinary frozen downstream computation; and causal
ablation removes 165 otherwise correct responses. The result is stronger than
showing that a Python calculator returns sums, but weaker than a general neural
calculator.

Limitations are substantial: one model revision, nonnegative one-to-four-digit
addition, explicit ABI supervision, adaptive engineering before the final
audit, runtime-managed state, and one call per response. A clean next test is
the identical protocol on a Llama-family model with untouched-base and
parameter-matched controls. Separately, a bounded controller with explicit
\texttt{IDLE}, \texttt{CAPTURE}, \texttt{EMIT}, and \texttt{RESET} states could
test multiple calls without unbounded loops. The current evidence supports
continued work on those interfaces; it does not establish spontaneous or
unlimited calculator use during arbitrary reasoning.

\section{Frozen gates and failure decomposition}

The final protocol required: (1) every seed at least 57/60 exact and mean at
least 58/60; (2) every seed at least 57/60 exact operands; (3) every seed at
least 50/60 paired ablation loss; (4) every seed at most 2/60 false routes and
at least 58/60 exact preservation; and (5) every active route with exact
operands to have an exact calculator trajectory and exact formatted answer.
Gates 2--4 passed. Gates 1 and 5 failed as described in the main text.

The exact-result ablation is applied at the 12 reserved result coordinates
while retaining the rest of the learned interface and frozen base. It leaves
3/60 answers correct in each seed. The paired losses are 54, 55, and 56 because
some examples are already wrong with the calculator active, while a small
number happen to remain or become correct under ablation.
